## 1. Summary and honest verdict

**What was taught.** The "untapped digital gold mines" are finally named: **the audiences of micro-creators (10k–100k
followers) who have no product of their own.** The method is a **creator partnership on revenue share**: you find a
creator, build a product in their name, voice and branding, publish it on Whop with an automatic split, and the creator
promotes it to their audience. This is what the Day 3 VIP called **borrowed value**, and what the Day 2 testimonials
hinted at ("I had 30% of that"). It is presented as **three switches**:
1. **Partner:** find creators on Instagram ("industry + keyword"), keep only those passing **four filters** (videos with
   50k–200k views; 10k–100k followers; no product of their own; business email in bio), and cold-email them at scale with
   an AI outreach tool (**ListKit**) until some reply "interested".
2. **Personalize:** paste the creator's Instagram or YouTube handle into **Synthesize**; it analyses niche, transformation,
   content themes, audience, voice and tone, proposes product ideas and builds a branded ebook.
3. **Publish:** generate the store page, price, icon and cover inside Synthesize, publish to **Whop**, send the link to the
   creator. Whop takes payment, delivers, and pays both parties their agreed share.

The guests (Roman, David) add the human side: **one creator is enough to start**, the first deal can be a **free month
for a revenue share**, pitching can be done creatively (they **booked a paid 1:1 session** with a psychologist to pitch
her), you copy-paste the same launch for more creators, referrals bring inbound, and non-English markets are underserved.
Mark Tilbury reframes it as "get to the front of an existing queue" and confirms from the creator's side that attention
"doesn't pay the mortgage".

**What was not taught (the hard parts):** how the creator list and emails were built, **any outreach email text**, the
follow-up after "interested" (call, pitch, negotiation), the **contract and split mechanics**, the **launch** itself (what
the creator posts, when, how often; Eileen Wilder's launch talk was cut from the recording), refunds and customer service,
and Whop fees.

## 2. Distribution method in full (as taught)

### 2.1 The idea: creators are stadiums without jerseys (≈ 01:11–01:30)

- **Rinse and repeat, not "hope marketing".** Random trend-chasing gives "feast or famine income". Instead, put the product
  "in the right place in front of the people who are already there to buy it"; then "demand is never your problem.
  Supply is" (supermarket shelf) (~01:11–01:16).
- **Branded beats plain.** Two identical jerseys; fans buy the club's, even at a higher price (Liverpool: "more than 1.8
  million jerseys… more than $130 million" in 2022–23). A club with no jerseys would sell out instantly to "presold
  buyers" (~01:16–01:19).
- **Online, the stadiums are creators.** "More than 41 million creators with anywhere from 10,000 to 100,000 followers just
  on Instagram"; "less than 1%" have a product. "There's no shortage of buyers. There's a shortage of products" (~01:19–01:21).
- **Trust makes it forgiving.** "When the audience already trusts the person who's making the recommendation, you can do
  pretty much everything wrong, and people will still buy" (~01:23).
- **Micro-creators, not famous ones.** Messages to big accounts get "buried under 500 other messages", filtered by
  managers and agencies, and big deals go to brands. Micro-creators (10k–100k) "answer their own comments… reply to their
  own DMs"; their audience feels it knows them (~01:24–01:27).
- **Why creators say yes.** Fans have paid them nothing "because there was never anything to buy". Linktree survey: "46%
  of full-time creators… earn less than $1,000 a year"; 68% of part-timers. They think of "sponsorships, brand deals, and
  affiliate links"; a **business-inquiries email in the bio** is "them literally raising their hand" (~01:27–01:30). Mark
  Tilbury (with "over 20 million followers"): "You can't pay your mortgage with attention… creating something people want
  to buy is a completely different skill"; creators lack time; "a person who comes to me with something genuinely valuable
  is far more interesting than someone who simply wants access to my audience" (~03:37–03:42).

### 2.2 Where and how to find creators (≈ 01:33–01:38)

1. **Instagram**, a **dummy account** is fine (no photo, no name).
2. Go to the **Explore page** and search **"industry + a keyword creators in that industry use"**: e.g. "parenting" +
   "tips" = **"parenting tips"**. The goal is a creator who attracts a profitable pocket (a specific person with a specific
   problem).
3. Scroll the results; **skip the top results with millions of views**.
4. Apply the four filters; a creator must pass **all four**, otherwise move on.

| # | Filter | Rule | Why (as said) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Video views** | A video doing **50,000–200,000 views** | "This creator can make content people actually want to watch", and it gets you "past the big accounts at the top" |
| 2 | **Followers** | **10,000–100,000** (shown at the top of the profile) | "Over 100,000 starts to get more competitive, and under 10,000, the audience is usually too small to make the numbers work" |
| 3 | **No own product** | No link to "her own course, ebook, or coaching program" in the bio, and no own product promoted in posts | "Infinitely easier to take someone from a 0 out of 10 to a 5 out of 10 than… a 5 out of 10… to an 8" |
| 4 | **Business email in bio** | An email "for business inquiries" | "She's open to partnerships. She is expecting people to reach out to her with business deals" |

Example: a female creator with **75,000 followers**, no product link, business email: passes all four.
Other platforms: Synthesize accepts **Instagram or YouTube** handles (~01:50); the guests also launched with a **Telegram**
channel (~03:07). No other discovery source (TikTok, databases, hashtags) was mentioned.

### 2.3 What one partnership is worth (EXAMPLE, ≈ 01:38–01:40)

$150 product × 75,000 followers × **0.5%** buyers = **375 buyers = $56,250**; split **50/50** → **$28,000** "from one single
product". "2 to 3% is normal in a lot of markets. So we are being ultra-conservative with 0.5%." See §8 for why these
numbers are not conservative.

### 2.4 Outreach process (≈ 01:40–01:44)

- **Don't** send generic messages ("Hey, you want to work together?"): they get ignored.
- **Do** use a "specialized AI" for cold email that sends at scale, measures opens, replies and positive/negative replies,
  and learns "what combinations of words" get positive replies. Follow-ups run automatically.
- Your job: "choose if you want them or not" when a creator replies "I want this".
- **Demo (ListKit, recorded):** see §4.1.
- **Scripts: none shown.** The only wording given is the anti-example above. The guests' verbal pitches (below) are the
  closest thing to a script.

**How the guests actually got creators (EXAMPLE, ≈ 02:49–03:04):**
- **Personal network:** David asked **his own fitness trainer** (2–3k Instagram followers): "How much do you need to pay
  you? I said, nothing… We work together for one month for free. And then if we make some money, we share the profit… I
  made videos for you. I made some reels for you." → fitness/nutrition product, **$3,000, his half $1,500**.
- **Manual cold messages:** Roman "was just messaging people… I just used to outreach": first launch ~$800, third $5,000.
  Later a cold message to a **5M-follower** creator in Kazakhstan → **$1.7M** launch, his part "almost $300,000".
- **Pay to get a meeting:** a psychologist selling 1:1 dating sessions for people around 40: "instead of doing cold
  outreach, we decided to book one-to-one session with her. We paid for the session and explained to her this
  opportunity… instead of doing one-to-one session, we can create you a product about how to date when you're 40" →
  **$600k**. "We know zero about relationships."
- **The pitch in all cases:** no money upfront, we build and run it, we share revenue.

### 2.6 Launch mechanics (as far as covered)

- Publish on Whop, **"copy the link… send it to the creator"**; the creator promotes it (~01:58).
- The product keeps selling "for as long as the creator keeps promoting this product to their audience" (Zada: €11k first
  month, then €6–8k/month "because the product was still sitting in front of that same audience every single day") (~01:21, ~01:46).
- Guests: "**multiple launches annually**" with one creator for "two years, three years"; the same launch, copy-pasted,
  made $3k with a 2–3k creator and €100k with a bigger one (~03:05).
- **Nothing on launch content, timing, pre-launch, live events, email, sales calls or pricing tactics.** The guests'
  numbers (€500 course, $1.7M, $400k from an 11k Telegram channel) point to **course launches**, not $45 ebooks.
- Eileen Wilder's "one-day cash machine" (presumably a one-day launch format) was promised as the companion to this
  distribution method but is **not in the recording**.

### 2.7 Scaling and other "gold mines" (≈ 03:04–03:09; Mark ~03:32–03:37)

- **Start with one creator** in your free time; take a second and third once income beats your salary; then "copy, paste,
  copy, paste".
- **Referrals:** creators "started talking to each other… and they started to recommend to me"; inbound follows a good launch.
- **1–3 good partners** are enough (David).
- **Small creators can work:** "a guy with 10K, 20K followers" is enough to learn; a Russian **Telegram** channel with
  **11,000** followers did **$400,000** cash collected.
- **Other languages and markets:** German, Italian, Spanish, Portuguese, Kazakh, Brazilian, Colombian, Argentinian markets
  have few people doing this; creators abroad reach out to proven operators.
- **Niches mentioned:** parenting, pediatrics, fitness/nutrition, marketing agencies, dating at 40, psychology, real estate,
  trading.
- **Mark's principle of early channels:** "Sometimes you don't need to find new customers at all. You just need to reach
  existing customers in a way your competitors haven't caught on to yet" (his mobile-optimised shop). No concrete channel named.
- **No other distribution channel** (marketplaces, SEO, ads, own content) was taught in this session.

## 5. Bestseller and "what sells" signals mentioned

- **The creator's audience is the demand signal:** Synthesize reads "what their audience is already begging to pay for"
  from content themes and audience (claim). Filter 1 (50k–200k views) = proof the creator's content is wanted.
- **Creators already selling services are proven demand:** the psychologist sold paid 1:1 sessions; turning that into a
  product made $600k (even though filter 3 says to skip such creators).
- **Price points heard:** $14 ebook (Zada, 70k audience); $29 (Millionaire Mindset, Iman's own audience); **$45** (demo);
  **$150–300** ("personalized digital products", claimed); **€500** course (David); high-ticket implied by $1.7M and $400k
  launches.
- **Niches of successful partnerships:** parenting (search example), pediatrics (Gael, Mexico), fitness and nutrition,
  marketing agency, dating at 40 / relationships, psychology, real estate, trading.
- **Markets:** Spanish-speaking (Spain, Colombia, Argentina), Italian, Russian (Telegram), Kazakh, Brazilian; "nobody" does
  this in German, Italian, Spanish or Portuguese (Roman, claim).
- **Synthesize "trained on over $155 million in digital product sales… knows exactly which products sell"** (pitch; no
  evidence; same claim as Day 2).
- **No bestseller data, marketplace or ranking** was shown. The only "what sells" method is: pick a creator whose audience
  trusts them, then build what that audience asks about.

## 10. **OUR INTERPRETATION:** implications for [`system/requirements.md`](../../system/requirements.md)

> ⚠ Our interpretation, not course content. Proposed changes only; `requirements.md` has **not** been edited.

### 10.1 What this session changes
- The course's distribution method is **B2B partnership sales**, not B2C marketing: the "customer" we must win is the
  **creator**; the creator's trust sells to the end buyer. Our system would be a **ghost producer** ("shadow operator").
- This is **semi-autonomous at best**: finding and qualifying creators and drafting outreach can be automated; replies,
  calls, negotiation, contracts and launch coordination need the operator. It conflicts with **N1** unless the operator
  accepts a few hours per active partner per week. Proposed: keep the creator-partner channel as **one of three channels**
  (partner, owned faceless channel, marketplaces from 03a §6.3) and compare cost per sale.
- The order flips: **creator first, product second**. Our research layers must also work in that direction (§10.3).

### 10.2 Distribution requirements (proposed R12a, partner channel)
- **R12a-1 Creator discovery.** Find creators by niche keywords on Instagram, YouTube, TikTok and Telegram **only through
  permitted means** (official APIs, licensed creator databases, manual operator search assisted by the system). No
  scraping against platform terms. Store public metrics with date and source.
- **R12a-2 Qualification score.** Start with the course's four filters as defaults (views 50k–200k, followers 10k–100k, no
  own product, business email), then add: engagement rate and comment quality, audience language/country, fit with a
  profitable pocket from R1, audience questions in comments, brand safety, fake-follower check, and for health, finance
  and psychology niches a **credential check** (the creator must be qualified). Treat the size band as adjustable (the
  guests succeeded at 2–3k and at 5M).
- **R12a-3 Outreach with approval.** The system drafts personalised emails (what we noticed in their content, the specific
  product idea, no-upfront-cost revenue share). The operator approves the template and **each batch**; low daily volume;
  opt-out in every email; suppression list; sending-domain hygiene. Respect CAN-SPAM, GDPR/ePrivacy and PECR (EU/UK
  creators are often sole traders, which limits cold email in some countries). Prefer the creator's published business
  channel.
- **R12a-4 Reply triage and pipeline.** Classify replies, draft answers for approval, track each creator through
  found → qualified → contacted → replied → call → agreed → building → launched → reporting.
- **R12a-5 Deal terms.** A lawyer-checked revenue-share agreement template: split (course default 50/50; seen 17–50%),
  term, exclusivity, IP ownership, approval of content, refunds and support, what happens if promotion stops, payout and tax
  forms. **Operator signs.**
- **R12a-6 Launch kit.** For each product, generate the creator's launch material (posts, stories, scripts, emails, FAQ,
  launch calendar) for the creator to approve. The course **did not teach launches**: research launch formats (creator
  launches, the Russian-speaking "producer" model the guests come from, webinars) before building this.
- **R12a-7 Partner reporting.** A shared sales view so the creator trusts the numbers (Whop dashboards or our own report).
- **R12a-8 Saturation check.** Before contacting a creator, check signs they are already approached or partnered (e.g. new
  product links); expect falling reply rates as course buyers flood the same pool.

### 10.3 The two research layers
- **R1 Demand, audience level:** in partner mode, demand comes from **one creator's audience**: recurring questions and
  complaints in comments, what they ask the creator, which videos get unusual views. Add "audience-question mining" for a
  given creator (with content they have shared or public comments where allowed).
- **R2 Bestsellers, creator level:** the creators that **fail filter 3** (already selling) are the best bestseller dataset
  for that niche: product type, price, platform (Stan, Beacons, Whop, Gumroad, Kajabi), launch style and sometimes public
  sales or review counts. Proposed **R2c:** "For each niche, record what comparable creators sell, at what price and on
  which platform." Also: creators selling **1:1 services** prove their audience pays (the psychologist case).
- **R4 Combine:** a partner opportunity = creator score × pocket demand × proof that similar audiences buy × our ability
  to build a credible product in that niche.
- **Economics:** model partnership revenue with conservative conversion ranges (e.g. 0.05–0.5% of followers), our share
  (17–50%), and the product price; rank by expected value per hour of operator time.

### 10.4 Creation (R9–R11) in partner mode
- **R9b Creator-sourced synthesis.** With permission, ingest the creator's content (transcripts, captions, posts) as the
  primary voice and method; keep external sources for accuracy and references. The creator reviews and approves the final
  product (R11).
- Default format stays the ebook MVP; the guests' results suggest **higher-priced courses or programmes** drive the big
  launches; keep video courses (slides + voiceover) on the roadmap for partners.

### 10.5 Delivery (R13)
- Add: **revenue split or partner payouts** (native split, collaborator feature, or the creator as a high-commission
  **affiliate** with their own link, which most platforms support and is simpler).
- Keep: merchant of record (VAT/sales tax), refunds, payouts to our country, account-freeze risk (03a). Evaluate Whop
  (fees never stated by the course) against Gumroad, Lemon Squeezy, Payhip, Stan.
- Add: **attribution** per creator (link or coupon) so each partner's sales are clear.

### 10.6 Tool questions for phase 2
1. Creator discovery: which official APIs (Instagram Graph *Business Discovery*, YouTube Data API, TikTok) and which
   licensed databases (Modash, HypeAuditor, Upfluence, Heepsy…) give follower, view and engagement data legally, and at what cost?
2. Business email: what can be collected and stored legally per country; verification tools.
3. Outreach: Claude-drafted emails sent via a sequencer (Instantly, Smartlead, lemlist) vs ListKit vs Gmail at low volume;
   deliverability setup; reply classification with Claude.
4. CRM for the partner pipeline: a sheet, Notion, Airtable or HubSpot free?
5. Creator content ingestion: YouTube transcripts, Whisper for reels, Instagram data export by the creator.
6. Store: which platform supports split payouts or affiliates, MoR, our payout country, lowest fees?
7. Contracts: a revenue-share template (legal review), e-signature.
8. Launch: what launch formats work for 10k–100k creators (and for Telegram), and what can we automate?

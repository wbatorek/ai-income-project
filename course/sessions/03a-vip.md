## 1. Summary

The session is about **packaging and selling without showing your face**.
1. **What buyers pay for.** Buyers "only care about two main things: the problem… and the fix". A digital product is
   "information that is synthesized into a method that achieves an outcome". The right question is not "who am I to sell
   this?" but "does it take someone from point A to point B?"
2. **Intrinsic vs borrowed value.** Intrinsic value lies in the thing itself (Google Maps, a wedding budget tracker, Avada,
   Easlo's templates). Borrowed value comes from a famous creator (a signed Ronaldo jersey costs more). Borrowed value is
   real but "the absolute minority"; "the whole industry basically runs on intrinsic value". Borrowed value will be taught
   in the main session.
3. **Four formats, all doable faceless:** ebooks (start here; AI makes testing topics fast), video programs (Loom or
   Slides + voiceover; avatars are poor value), digital assets ("actively used", the best faceless format), digital minds
   (an agent trained on a content corpus, no coding).
4. **Hosting (Whop, co-owned):** AI-built store page, every format, one-time / subscription / instalment payments,
   automatic access, sales dashboard, 241 countries; communities, coaching, iFrame embeds. "A simple payment process…
   makes a huge, huge difference on the amount of sales."
5. **Getting bought = standing out:** first the right problem and a real fix; second, a trustworthy, premium look, with AI
   doing ~90% of the design and a human checking. A further "hidden method" is saved for the main session.
6. **Case study (Michal):** ChatGPT ebook → PayPal froze and banned him → Whop. Raised his ebook + 1:1 mentorship from
   $200 to $1,500 and sales got *easier*. Grew a faceless Instagram with daily black-background text cards; his followers'
   questions showed him the demand; later sold done-for-you ebooks to followers.
7. **Homework:** pick a format, write point A and point B, post it in the Whop.

## 2. Key teachings

### 2.1 Intrinsic vs borrowed value (≈ 00:06–00:14)

| | Intrinsic value | Borrowed value |
|---|---|---|
| Where the value sits | "In the thing itself, not in the person that's endorsing it or the person that created it" | In the creator's name or fame |
| Analogy | Google Maps: you never ask who mapped the roads; a wedding budget tracker bought on Google for "a couple of dollars" | Two identical Ronaldo #7 jerseys; the signed one sells for more |
| Share of the market | "The whole industry basically runs on intrinsic value, and that is where most of the sales are actually made" | "The absolute minority" of a "$250–300B" industry (figure from memory) |
| What it allows | Faceless, no following, weak English, any country: "completely irrelevant" | A higher price ("you would probably understand why the price is a little bit higher") |
| Examples | Avada (ThemeForest), Visualize Value, Easlo, Getting Real | Experts you follow; to be taught in the main session |

**The test question:** "Whatever I'm going to create… does it take someone from point A to point B? Does it fix the
problem? If it does that, then it doesn't matter who you are."

### 2.2 The four formats and how to choose (≈ 00:14–00:23)

| Format | What it is | How to make it faceless | Example given | Claimed numbers |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Ebook** | "Basically just a PDF"; 20 or 200 pages "doesn't really matter too much"; the course workbooks combined would be one | Naturally faceless | *Getting Real*, 37signals (2006), on building web apps | $19, ~5,750 copies, $120k in 30 days |
| **Video program** | Modules "like chapters of a book", from introductory to "the more advanced strategies at the end" | **Loom** screen recording (camera off), **Google Slides + voiceover**; **Higgsfield** AI avatars possible but "the bang for buck isn't quite there" | Josh Comeau, *CSS for JavaScript Developers* (2021), **pre-sold before it was finished**; the company's own $25k enterprise course "was actually done in Loom" | ~$120, 4,800 pre-orders, $577k in a week |
| **Digital asset** | "Not just information-based… something that people actively use, which makes the faceless element absolutely massive" | Inherently faceless (cartoon avatar, made-up brand name) | Easlo (Notion templates, "second brain"); Avada (website template) | Easlo $239k in a year, $500k+ total; Avada 800k sales, $60M |
| **Digital mind** | An AI agent "trained on whatever content you really want", so it answers like that person | No coding; "platforms" (not named) build it; embed on Whop via iFrame | "Ask Iman" mind *[?]* on 10 years of Iman's content, programmes, team-meeting transcripts and coaching calls | None |

**How to choose (what was actually said):**
- **Ebook = default starting point**: "the starting point for most people", "very easy way to get started", and with AI
  "you can start testing… topics and offers very, very quickly".
- **Asset = strongest faceless format** (presenter's favourite): used, not just read.
- **Video**: common and lucrative; production quality matters far less than value ("you'll be surprised how little that
  actually matters… this is coming from the chief product officer").
- **Digital mind**: newest, "most groundbreaking"; claimed not technical.
- **No selection criteria beyond that.** Format fit to problem, price per format, or which format sells best was not taught.
  Homework asks you to pick one, and rewards finding "other formats".

### 2.3 Point A → point B

Repeated as the definition of a product ("something that can take someone from point A to point B") and as the first

**Platform risk (EXAMPLE):** Michal's PayPal account "was good for a week", was frozen and then **permanently banned**, with
money stuck; he moved to Whop.

### 2.5 Getting bought: standing out (≈ 00:28–00:30)

1. **The right problem, actually fixed.** "If you're fixing the right problem, you automatically start to stand out…
   Taking someone from point A to point B is the number one thing."
2. **How it looks.** "Often a sale is made based on trust… the way that your digital product is represented and looks is
   a part of that." Premium product → premium branding.
3. **AI does the design.** "AI should be doing 90% of the work when it comes to design, especially in the early stages,
   and you should just be checking it."
4. **Deferred:** a "hidden method" for standing out (main session).

### 2.6 Distribution signals (not taught as a method; all from examples)

- **Daily faceless content → audience → product.** Visualize Value: one simple black-and-white graphic a day, later
  courses and a one-page planner, $1M+ in 2020 "without spending any money on acquisition, any money on ads". Michal:
  black background, white text, motivational cards (ChatGPT + Canva) **every day for three months**, then one reel went
  viral (7–13M views).
- **The audience tells you what to sell.** Followers asked "how are you growing your page?", so his first product was
  Instagram growth. Later he posted in his stories "who needs ebooks or digital products, just DM me" and sold
  done-for-you ebooks (ADHD ebook, $67).
- **Marketplaces.** Avada is the best seller *on ThemeForest*; nobody knew the creators.
- **Pre-sales.** Josh Comeau sold 4,800 pre-orders for an unfinished course.
- **Worldwide and any language.** Michal's buyers: New Zealand, US, Dubai, India. Spanish speakers can sell in Spanish;
  "a mom of two" sells in the Lithuanian market (claim: 150k/year). "They won't ask you, where are you from?"

### 2.7 What sells best (as far as the session says)

- No ranking of formats or niches by sales was given.
- The examples are all **intrinsic-value products with public sales numbers**: a marketplace best seller (template), a
  practical how-to ebook for professionals, a skills course for developers, productivity templates.
- **Higher price can sell more easily** (Michal: at $200 "people were asking too many questions"; at $1,500 "they paid.
  That's it"). Andres: "you actually get easier clients" and buyers are "actually valuing the offer". The next VIP is to
  cover "increasing value… you can actually increase the price and make more sales".

## 4. Claims vs evidence

| Claim | Assessment |
|---|---|
| Intrinsic value dominates; famous creators are "the absolute minority" | Plausible for templates, tools and marketplaces; unproven for courses and info products, where creator brands are prominent. The course itself will teach borrowed value next, so even they don't treat it as negligible |
| Industry "$250–300B" | From memory ("I can't remember the exact number"). Market-size estimates vary widely with definition (e-learning, software, media). Rhetoric |
| Avada: 800k sales, $60M, "two-person team" | Avada is a long-standing ThemeForest best seller and ThemeForest shows public sales counts, so this is **checkable**. Revenue is gross before Envato's large cut; "two-person team" is doubtful today. Verify in `research/` |
| Visualize Value: $58 → $1M+ in 2020, no ads | Matches what Jack Butcher has said publicly; self-reported. He built the audience for years before (not a quick win) |
| Getting Real: $19, ~5,750 copies, $120k/30 days | 5,750 × $19 ≈ $109k, not $120k; 37signals already had a large audience (borrowed value, in fact). Self-reported |
| Josh Comeau: ~$120, 4,800 pre-orders, $577k/week | Arithmetic is consistent. He had a large developer following and blog (again borrowed value). Self-reported |
| Easlo: $239k/year (Business Insider), $500k+ total | Plausible and publicly reported; real name "Jason Chin" unverified. A strong faceless case |
| $25k enterprise course "all done in Loom" | Internal, unverified; buyers of a $25k offer likely paid for access and coaching, not the video quality |
| Whop: "free to start", 241 countries, payments "optimise" sales | Co-owner speaking. Whop takes a per-sale fee (host's own disclosure, 01-opp ~01:24). Compare with Gumroad, Lemon Squeezy, Payhip, Stan etc. in `research/` |
| "A simple payment process makes a huge difference" | Standard checkout-conversion wisdom; plausible; no data given |
| AI should do 90% of design | Reasonable for MVPs; Higgsfield named as a design tool (it is mainly an image/video generator) |
| Michal: "50,000, 60,000" in a year | Earlier: "$60k total" (01-opp ~01:51) and "about to hit $100k" (02b-main ~03:49). Inconsistent; most of it appears to come from **1:1 mentorship**, not a faceless digital product |
| $200 → $1,500 made sales easier | One anecdote; a 7.5× price jump on an offer that **adds 1:1 time** (more value, not the same offer). "You don't even have to change the offer" contradicts his own account that he bundled mentorship |
| Lithuanian mom, 150k/year on the side | No evidence |
| 6 months of consistency = "95%" chance | Rhetoric; survivor bias |
| Synthesize: draft in 1–2 min | Consistent with 02b-main demo; still 2–3 days total with edits for client work |

**Sales framing to note:**
- **Conflict of interest:** the entire hosting segment is a co-owner recommending his own platform.
- **Two teasers** for the main session ("borrowed value", "hidden method") and one for tomorrow's VIP (pricing).
- **Authority priming** of the presenter by Iman; the guest's success story is told as the payoff of "trusting the advice" of the paid coaching.
- **Faceless is the theme, but** the most profitable item in the case study is Michal's own **1:1 time**, and he is now
  revealing his face. Faceless works, but the course's own example shows personal involvement pays more.

## 6. **OUR INTERPRETATION:** implications for [`system/requirements.md`](../../system/requirements.md)

> ⚠ Our interpretation, not course content. Proposed changes only; `requirements.md` has **not** been edited.

### 6.1 Two research layers: demand + bestsellers (R1–R4)
- **R2 Bestseller research gets concrete sources.** Every example in this session came from a place that **shows sales
  publicly** or from a self-published revenue report: ThemeForest (public sales counts per item), Notion template
  marketplaces, Gumroad/Whop pages, creators' own "how I made $X" posts (Easlo, Josh Comeau, Jack Butcher), press articles.
  Proposed: **R2a** "Collect public sales signals (sales counters, review counts, rank, creator revenue posts) per
  marketplace and store them with date and source, so trends can be measured over time."
- **R2b Intrinsic vs borrowed filter.** When we learn from a bestseller, tag whether its sales came from the **product**
  (marketplace best seller, unknown creator) or from the **creator's audience** (37signals, Josh Comeau). Only
  intrinsic-value bestsellers are good templates for a faceless system. Getting Real and Josh Comeau were sold as
  intrinsic-value proof but are borrowed-value cases.
- **R1 Demand research gets a new signal:** **questions directed at an account or community** ("how are you doing X?",
  "can I get this?"). Michal's product came from his DMs. For us: recurring questions in comments, replies and community
  threads in a niche are a primary demand source.
- **R4 Combine layers:** keep demand + proof of payment + gap; add **pre-sale as the cheapest proof of payment** (Josh
  Comeau). Proposed: optional pre-sale or waitlist step before full creation, behind an operator gate (R15).

### 6.2 Formats (R10)
- **Format becomes a scored dimension**, not just "ebook first". Proposed criteria per format: automation fit, faceless
  fit, perceived value / price ceiling, production cost, refund risk, platform support.
  - **Ebook/PDF:** fastest to test topics; stays the default MVP.
  - **Digital asset (template, tracker, planner, Notion/Sheets):** "actively used", best faceless format; strong
    intrinsic value; the wedding tracker and Easlo examples suggest **cheap, practical tools** sell on search and
    marketplaces. Promote to an early, not late, format in R10.
  - **Video program:** can be fully generated as **slides + AI voiceover** or screen recordings (no avatars needed).
    Feasible for the system later; higher price point.
  - **Digital mind:** a chatbot over a curated corpus, embedded by iFrame. Technically feasible (RAG), but adds ongoing
    hosting/LLM cost, accuracy risk and support. Phase-3 candidate only.
  - **Coaching / 1:1 bundles** (Michal's $1,500): **out of scope** for an autonomous system (fails the stop test and N1).
- **Format research question for R2:** which formats sell best *per niche* is not answered by the course; the bestseller
  layer must measure it.

### 6.3 Distribution (R12) and sales (R13)
- **Faceless daily-content channel** is the only distribution method the course has shown so far (Visualize Value,
  Michal): simple text or graphic posts, every day, for months, then products for the audience that forms. It is highly
  automatable (generate + schedule) but slow (months) and subject to platform rules on automation and AI content.
  Proposed **R12a**: "Optional owned faceless channel per niche, with drafted posts the operator approves; measure follower
  questions as demand input (feeds R1)."
- **Marketplace distribution** (ThemeForest-style best sellers) fits intrinsic-value products: buyers search the
  marketplace, the creator is irrelevant. Proposed **R12b**: "Prefer channels where buyers browse and search for the
  product type (marketplaces), alongside communities."
- **Wait for the main session** for the actual distribution method (the "hidden method", borrowed value, "gold mines").
- **R13 Payments:** add **payment-processor risk** (Michal's PayPal ban with funds stuck) and **instalment/subscription
  support** as selection criteria; keep the merchant-of-record requirement. Evaluate Whop on fees and terms, not on the
  course's recommendation.
- **New: localisation.** The course claims any language works (Spanish, Lithuanian). Proposed **R10b**: "Products and
  listings can be generated in other languages; research can target non-English markets with less competition", gated
  by the operator's ability to check quality (R11).
- **Presentation and trust (R11):** design is part of conversion. Add "AI-generated cover, mockups and landing page with
  human check" to the creation pipeline.
- **Pricing (R8):** add price **testing including higher price points**, and record that price changes perceived value.
  Wait for tomorrow's VIP (pricing) before designing the price ladder.

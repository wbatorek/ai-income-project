## 1. What counts as a digital product

- **Two rules, and only two:** it is **developed digitally** and **delivered digitally** (02a-vip ~00:09; 01-opp ~01:49).
  - "It needs to be created and delivered completely digitally. That's it." (01-opp ~01:49)
  - "No physical shipping. No inventory. Just information delivered through your computer." (01-opp ~01:49)
- **Passes:** Kindle book, Notion template, collection of woodworking plans (01-opp ~01:49); World of Warcraft guide, plant-based cookbook (02a-vip ~00:09).
- **Fails:** a DVD of movies. It can be developed digitally but has to be shipped physically (02a-vip ~00:09).
- **Typical shape:** "a downloadable piece of content… a PDF, a guide, a template, or a curated resource. The format doesn't really matter. What matters is, does it solve a problem?" (01-opp ~01:48)
- **Length doesn't define it:** like a recipe book, "it doesn't really matter if it's 1 page long or 20 pages long" (01-opp ~01:48). See §5 for the contradiction with the ~45-page standard.

## 2. Why digital products work (economics)

- **Recipe, not cake.** A cake needs ingredients, kitchenware and baking time for every unit; a recipe is created once and sold "how many ever times you want" (02a-vip ~00:11). Same idea with "a recipe book" (01-opp ~01:48).
- **Built once, sold infinitely:** "a PDF that was created once and can be sold an infinite number of times" (01-opp ~01:49).
- **Same work for 10 or 10,000 sales:** no inventory or shipping (shoes counter-example); at most "a few more emails" (02a-vip ~00:12; 01-opp ~02:24).
- **The value is in the product, not the creator.** "Nobody cares who compiled the top woodworking plans. They care about the woodworking plans themselves" (01-opp ~02:09). "People don't care who you are… does it fix the problem that I have?" (02a-vip ~00:17). Hence faceless products work (01-opp ~02:08). The Day 3 VIP calls this **intrinsic value** (§2.1) (03a-vip ~00:09).
- **Small numbers are enough:** 37 buyers × $250/month ≈ $9k/month (01-opp ~01:55; 02a-vip ~00:18). "You only need a few dozen people buying your product" (02a-vip ~00:18).
- **No ongoing money cost, unlike physical products:** "For beginners specifically, I am not a fan of anything physical product related because you're going to either have to burn money on ads or burn money on like the physical product"; Iman's own shipment was stuck for three and a half months in 2020; with digital products "it doesn't cost more to keep trying" (04a-vip ~00:32–00:33). Rule: "do something that doesn't cost you money ongoing… every month you're not spending money on ads or stock" (04a-vip ~00:48).
- **Buyers want the result, not "authenticity":** "If you have a problem, like do you care that the answer is authentic…? People just care, does it give me a result?" (04a-vip ~00:30).
- **Niches that look absurd are fine:** "Who would buy a guide on World of Warcraft?… trust me, there's a lot of people in this world" (02a-vip ~00:18). "Even if they look very, very specific, that's actually a good thing" (02a-vip ~00:18).

### 2.1 Intrinsic vs borrowed value (03a-vip ~00:06–00:14)

- **What a product is:** "something that can take someone from point A to point B"; "information that is synthesized into a method that achieves an outcome". "Buyers only care about 2 main things… the problem that they're currently facing and the fix" (03a-vip ~00:06–00:07).
- **Intrinsic value:** "the value of something lies in the thing itself, not in the person that's endorsing it or the person that created it" (03a-vip ~00:09). Google Maps: you never ask who mapped the roads (~00:11). A wedding budget tracker found on Google, bought for "a couple of dollars" without a thought about its maker (~00:07).
- **Borrowed value:** value that comes from the creator. Two identical Ronaldo #7 jerseys, the signed one costs more; a product by an expert you follow can charge "a little bit higher" (03a-vip ~00:09–00:10).
- **Which dominates:** borrowed value is "the absolute minority"; "the whole industry basically runs on intrinsic value, and that is where most of the sales are actually made" (industry sized from memory at "250, 300 billion") (03a-vip ~00:10). Borrowed value is to be taught in the main Episode 3 session (~00:10).
- **Consequence:** no face, no following, weak English, living outside the US/UK are "completely irrelevant" when the value is intrinsic: "the product is completely disconnected from who you are" (03a-vip ~00:13). Buyers "won't ask you, where are you from?"; any language works (Spanish, Lithuanian market) (03a-vip ~00:43–00:44).
- **The right question:** not "who am I to sell this?" but "does it take someone from point A to point B? Does it fix the problem?" (03a-vip ~00:08).

> Our note: two of the "intrinsic value" proofs (Getting Real by 37signals, Josh Comeau's course) were sold to large existing audiences, i.e. partly borrowed value. Avada, Easlo and the wedding tracker are the cleaner faceless cases.

### 2.2 Borrowed value as taught in the main session (03b-main ~01:16–01:23, ~01:47–01:48)

- **The branded jersey:** two identical jerseys; fans buy the club-branded one "even considering that the club's jersey costs more" (~01:17).
- **A personalised product** is the branded jersey: made for one creator's audience "in the creator's tone, voice, and branding", so "they recognize it as theirs" (~01:48). A generic product is "a plain white jersey" (~01:47).
- **Trust carries the sale:** Millionaire Mindset ($29, 14,000+ buyers, $370k) sold because "I have an audience that deeply trusts me… who trusted a recommendation from me enough to pull out their credit card on the spot" (~01:23).
- **Faceless still works,** because the creator, not the seller, is the face (~01:22). See [distribute.md](distribute.md).

### 3.2 Format rules

- **You don't need to pick a format yet.** "It doesn't truly matter what the format is" (02a-vip ~00:14).
- **Start with the simplest:** ebook or digital asset (02a-vip ~00:15). In the demo, ebook is chosen because "it's the easiest to validate the idea… if you find gold, you can keep digging. But let's find the gold first" (02b-main ~01:45).
- **Upgrade path, only after sales:** ebook → pre-recorded course → digital mind. "You only add complexity on top of it once simple is already working" (02a-vip ~00:15).
- **Default MVP:** a **~45-page PDF** instead of a 5-hour course (02b-main ~01:41). Details in [develop.md](develop.md).
- **Ebook = the testing format.** With AI "you can start testing, you know, topics and offers very, very quickly" (03a-vip ~00:16).
- **Digital assets are the strongest faceless format** because they are used, not just read (03a-vip ~00:20).
- **Production polish matters little.** "You guys will be surprised how little that actually matters… this is coming from the chief product officer"; buyers "cared about… the actual value" (03a-vip ~00:17–00:18).
- **You can pre-sell an unfinished product** "if… the value of the product is really there" ("a little bit more like advanced strategy") (03a-vip ~00:17).
- **Other formats are welcome:** the Day 3 homework asks for one of the four, with credit for finding others (03a-vip ~00:22, ~00:31). Coaching can be bundled or even be "your full digital product" (03a-vip ~00:27).
- **For a creator launch, start with an ebook:** "the easiest one to launch with, by far… we want the highest chance of you making your first side income dollars online as quick as humanly possible" (03b-main ~01:52).
- **No further selection criteria** (format vs problem, price per format, which format sells best) have been taught so far. The guests' big launches were **courses** (e.g. a €500 marketing-agency course) (03b-main ~02:56, PROOF).

## 5. Price points and models heard so far

- One-off: **$19** (credit cards), **$29** (Millionaire Mindset), **$20–$189** (Jonathan) (01-opp ~02:08; 02b-main ~01:19, ~01:32).
- Subscription: **€25/month** (Pokémon), **$250/month** (Fortnite maps) (01-opp ~01:55).
- One-off (Day 3 VIP examples): **$19** (*Getting Real*), **~$120** (Josh Comeau), "a couple of dollars" (wedding tracker) (03a-vip ~00:07, ~00:15, ~00:16).
- Commission / done-for-you: **~$70** / **$67** for an ADHD ebook (02b-main ~03:44; 03a-vip ~00:46).
- Bundle with 1:1 time: **$1,500** (Michal); enterprise video course **$25,000** (03a-vip ~00:18, ~00:39).
- Creator-partner products (Day 3 main): **$45** in the publishing demo (03b-main ~01:57); **$150** as "the low end of what these personalized digital products actually sell for" (EXAMPLE, ~01:38); "$150 to $300" (PITCH, ~02:08); a **$14** ebook (Zada) and a **€500** course (David) (PROOF, ~01:21, ~02:56).
- Eileen Wilder's first ebook: **$7** (MOTIVATION, 04b-main ~04:12).
- **No pricing method has been taught** in Episodes 1–2, and none in Episode 4 main either (only "people buy what they want the most", [buyer-psychology.md](buyer-psychology.md) §5b). The Day 3 VIP gave one anecdote: raising Michal's offer from $200 to $1,500 made sales easier, "you actually get easier clients" and buyers "are actually valuing the offer" (03a-vip ~00:38–00:39). The Day 4 VIP is to cover increasing price and value "where you actually don't have to drop the price to make more sales" (03a-vip ~00:38). It did not: the Episode 4 VIP was a Q&A with no pricing content (04a-vip).

## 6. Contradictions between sessions

- **Length:** "1 page or 20 pages doesn't matter" (01-opp ~01:48); 20 or 200 pages "doesn't really matter too much" (03a-vip ~00:15); a 20-page (or 5-page) ebook often beats 200 pages (02a-vip ~00:20, ~00:40) **vs** the ~45-page PDF as the standard MVP (02b-main ~01:15, ~01:41) and "summarise it into 40 pages" (02b-main ~01:36).
- **Number of formats:** four (02a-vip ~00:13) **vs** five in the Synthesize tool (02b-main ~01:45).
- **The 37 × $250 example:** Fortnite maps (01-opp ~01:55) **vs** "I think it was Fortnite maps or was the Pokémon cards" (02a-vip ~00:18). Episode 1 also gives its total as $127k (~01:55) and $126k (~02:09).
- **Digital mind difficulty:** "probably the most complex" format, to be added last (02a-vip ~00:15) **vs** "not a technical thing at all… platforms… allow you to make this" (03a-vip ~00:22).
- **Does the creator matter?** "People don't care who you are" (02a-vip ~00:17); the industry "runs on intrinsic value" (03a-vip ~00:10) **vs** borrowed value earns a premium and will be taught in the main session (03a-vip ~00:09–00:10), and the Day 3 case study's best-paid offer is Michal's own 1:1 time (03a-vip ~00:38), and the Day 3 main session's whole method is borrowed value through a creator's audience (03b-main ~01:16–01:22; §2.2).
- **Price of a creator product:** "$150… on the low end" (03b-main ~01:38) **vs** $45 in the demo (~01:57) and the $14 ebook of the lead case study (~01:21).
- **Weight loss:** called a weak niche (02a-vip ~00:28) but used as a good example (4-week weight-loss plan, 02a-vip ~00:40; postpartum weight loss, 02b-main ~01:31). See [choosing-products.md](choosing-products.md) §7.

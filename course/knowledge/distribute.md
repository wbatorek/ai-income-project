## 1. Distribution, not selling (01-opp ~02:14–02:16; 03b-main ~01:11–01:16)

- "Forget the word selling… you don't need to sell to be successful. What you need instead is something called **distribution**" (01-opp ~02:15).
- **Supermarket analogy:** cereal brands never cold-call you. They "position their products in a place where most people go. Where people are already predisposed to buy what they sell… They're just putting themselves in front of **presold buyers**" (01-opp ~02:15).
- Done right, "you don't need to sell, but you still end up selling thousands of times more than the other people who are focused on sales" (01-opp ~02:15).
- **Rinse and repeat, not "hope marketing".** Random acts of marketing on the latest trends give "feast or famine income". You need "a rinse and repeat process for getting your AI digital product in front of as many predisposed buyers as possible" (03b-main ~01:12–01:13).
- **Demand vs supply:** "When your product is positioned in the right place in front of the people who are already there to buy it… demand is never your problem. Supply is." A gold mine is a place where "the distribution is already proven… The only thing missing is a product on the shelf" (03b-main ~01:15).
- **Front of the queue (Mark Tilbury):** he bought a running shop instead of opening one, i.e. "access to customers"; "The fastest way to an income isn't starting a queue. It's getting in front of one" (03b-main ~03:16–03:31).

## 2. The gold mines: micro-creators' audiences (03b-main ~01:16–01:30)

- **Branded beats plain:** two identical jerseys; fans buy the club-branded one even at a higher price. A club with no jerseys would sell out to "presold buyers" (03b-main ~01:16–01:19).
- **Creators are stadiums without jerseys:** "more than 41 million creators with anywhere from 10,000 to 100,000 followers just on Instagram"; "less than 1%" have a product. "There's no shortage of buyers. There's a shortage of products" (03b-main ~01:19–01:20).
- **Trust is what sells:** "When the audience already trusts the person who's making the recommendation, you can do pretty much everything wrong, and people will still buy" (Millionaire Mindset, $29, 14k buyers, sold to Iman's own audience) (03b-main ~01:23).
- **Micro-creators, not famous ones:** messages to big accounts are "buried under 500 other messages" and filtered by managers and agencies; big deals go to brands. **A micro-creator has 10,000–100,000 followers**, still "answer[s] their own comments… reply to their own DMs", and their audience feels it knows them (03b-main ~01:24–01:27).
- **Why creators say yes:** their fans have paid them nothing "because there was never anything to buy"; Linktree survey: 46% of full-time and 68% of part-time creators earn under $1,000 a year; they think of "sponsorships, brand deals, and affiliate links". A **business-inquiries email in the bio** is "them literally raising their hand" (03b-main ~01:27–01:29).
- **From the creator's side (Mark Tilbury, 20M+ followers):** "You can't pay your mortgage with attention… creating something people want to buy is a completely different skill from creating videos people want to watch"; creators lack the time to build products; "a person who comes to me with something genuinely valuable is far more interesting than someone who simply wants access to my audience" (03b-main ~03:37–03:42).
- **Creators lack monetisation skills (guests):** "all creators [are] mostly creative minds… don't have any strategy… don't know how to monetize it"; big creators sell brand deals cheaply (03b-main ~03:08).

## 3. The method: three switches (03b-main ~01:30–01:59)

1. **Partner** with a creator to access their audience.
2. **Personalize** the product for that creator (their knowledge, voice and branding): see [develop.md](develop.md) §5.2.
3. **Publish** on a platform that handles payment, delivery and the revenue split: see [deliver.md](deliver.md) §3.

Claimed benefits: no contacts needed, no niche expertise ("the creator is the expert"), no website or tech (03b-main ~01:31, ~01:48).

### 3.1 Finding creators (03b-main ~01:33–01:38)

1. Open **Instagram** (a **dummy account** with no photo or name is fine) → **Explore**.
2. Search **industry + a keyword that creators in that industry use**: "parenting" + "tips" = **"parenting tips"**. You are looking for a creator who attracts a profitable pocket.
3. Scroll past the top results with millions of views.
4. Apply **all filters**; if one fails, skip. The session named four; **Workbook 3 lists five**, adding an engagement filter (WB3 p.19–20).

| # | Filter | Rule | Reason given |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Video views | **50,000–200,000** on a video | Content people want to watch; gets you past the big accounts |
| 2 | Followers | **10,000–100,000** | "Over 100,000 starts to get more competitive… under 10,000, the audience is usually too small to make the numbers work" |
| 3 | No own product | No course, ebook or coaching link in the bio, none promoted in posts | "Infinitely easier to take someone from a 0 out of 10 to a 5 out of 10" |
| 4 | Business email | An email "for business inquiries" in the bio | "She's open to partnerships" |
| 4b (WB3) | **Engagement** | Average views on the **last 10 Reels ≥ followers ÷ 10**; most of them must clear it | "One video can go viral by luck" (WB3 p.20) |

Worked example: a 75,000-follower creator, no product link, business email: passes (03b-main ~01:36–01:38).
Synthesize also accepts **YouTube** handles (03b-main ~01:50); the guests also used a **Telegram** channel (03b-main ~03:07).

**Market size claims (WB3 p.2, p.11):** 303 million creators worldwide; 41 million micro-creators on
Instagram; under 1% have a digital product. Linktree survey: 46% of full-time and 68% of part-time
creators earn under $1,000 a year. A business email in the bio is "them raising their hand".

### 3.2 Outreach (03b-main ~01:40–01:44)

- **Don't** send generic messages ("Hey, you want to work together?"): they get ignored (03b-main ~01:41).
- **Do** use a specialised cold-email tool that sends at scale, measures opens and positive/negative replies, and learns which wording works; it sends follow-ups by itself; you "choose if you want them or not" when a creator replies (03b-main ~01:41–01:42).
- **Demo (ListKit):** 100 creators already loaded in a campaign → save → auto-written sequence with follow-ups and A/B variants → default settings → publish → master inbox with replies marked "interested" (03b-main ~01:42–01:44). The list building and the email texts were not shown.
- **No outreach script was given.**
- **The best guest did it by hand:** James signed all his creators with manual outreach, before the automated "kit" existed: "I had to do it all myself" (04a-vip ~00:52). Getting the creator is "the first thing" a beginner works on (04a-vip ~00:13).
- **Video messages:** Loom, "an app where you can… record a video, and send it… **You guys will be using it to send messages to creators**" (Ali, 04b-main ~05:25; said in a MOTIVATION segment, no script or example shown). Ali got his own start by sending Iman a Loom video.
- **Why creators say yes (as pitched):** without a product "their total profit is zero", so a ready product is "one of the most valuable assets that they currently don't have" (PITCH, 04b-main ~02:23).
- **Scripts exist but weren't shown:** "Do the teams teach us to convince the creators to work with us? Yeah, absolutely"; "Do you have email scripts or call scripts that we can use? Yeah, we help you with all that once you come in" (PITCH, 04b-main ~05:08–05:10).

## 4. Launching and scaling (03b-main; 04a-vip)

- **Launch step as shown:** publish, "copy the link… send it to the creator" (~01:58). (A PROOF segment adds that sales continue "for as long as the creator keeps promoting this product" (~01:46).)
- **One creator first:** "you don't need to have 20 clients, 40 clients like in a marketing agency… with one client, you can make a lot of money" (~02:50). Take a second and third once it beats your salary (~03:05).
- **Copy-paste the launch:** "they're all the same. You just copy, paste the same algorithm"; the same launch made $3k with a 2–3k creator and €100k with a bigger one, "just because the influencer was bigger" (~03:05–03:06).
- **Several launches a year with one partner,** for "two years, three years"; the partner "can bring the friends of his" (~03:05).
- **Referrals:** creators "started talking to each other… and they started to recommend to me"; 1–3 good partners are enough (~03:06).
- **Small creators can work:** "a guy with 10K, 20K followers" is enough to learn; a Russian Telegram channel with 11,000 followers did $400,000 cash collected (~03:07).
- **Other languages are underserved:** German, Italian, Spanish, Portuguese ("Nobody"); launches in Spain, Colombia, Argentina, Italy, Kazakhstan, Brazil (~03:04, ~03:08).
- **Early channels (Mark Tilbury):** "You just need to reach existing customers in a way your competitors haven't caught on to yet" (his mobile-optimised shop) (~03:32–03:37).
- **The work changes as you grow:** first "getting the creator"; later "a lot more time working with the creators and doing maybe more complicated launches"; "there's many ways that you can launch… things that are easy and… more advanced" (04a-vip ~00:13–00:15).
- **Launches often land below projection (James):** "the projections were 15 grand and you guys only made three grand"; "we're projecting to make 20 and… they make maybe five". He has "never had something go so catastrophic… we make zero dollars". "Most of the time people are pretty understanding… they also want to make money, your partners"; "just being able to pick up the pieces is honestly the hardest thing" (04a-vip ~00:43–00:46).
- **The operator's job is relationship management:** "the majority of my job is like just managing things… managing my clients… situations and fires" (04a-vip ~00:41). What AI can't do: "client relations… a lot of my job is like being a therapist for my clients… this is the next step… we need to position the product this way… human-to-human interaction" (04a-vip ~00:49).
- **Capacity limit:** one operator let two of nine creators go as "too much" (04a-vip ~00:51).
- **Skills no longer needed (James):** "it used to be you have to have some sort of technical level… know a lot more about like marketing… some paid stuff", now "quite simple" with tools and AI (04a-vip ~00:41).
- **Copy is needed throughout a partnership:** "when these creators post about these AI digital products, like, what do they need to say?… Do they need to follow a specific script?… what if they need to send emails? Or… record videos about the digital product?… whenever you are working on partnerships or on your own digital products, there will be places where you will need to fill in a few words." Two options: write it yourself or hire a copywriter ("a couple thousand dollars up to a couple million") (04b-main ~02:07; set-up for a tool pitch, no wording taught).
- **Launch content, timing and format were not taught.** Episode 4 main added only story patterns for launch emails and live events (§4c).
- **Expect months, not weeks, to a first dollar:** "I think you have to prepare for a situation where maybe for nine months you don't make your first dollar… in this model, you can fail for nine months" (Iman, LESSON, 04b-main ~03:12). Contrast the pitch's "ready to test this week" and the 60-day insurance (PITCH, ~02:15, ~02:20); see §7.
- **Building it beside a job:** "90 minutes to maximum if you really want to push it three hours a day"; a job "gives you the security"; take the side thing main "when the time is right". More free time doesn't mean more output: after dropping out, Iman "had the whole day to myself, I became less efficient" (04b-main ~02:59–03:03).
- **A first launch, in numbers (PROOF):** Tashriq's first launch did "just under about $6,600" in sales (04b-main ~04:00).

## 4c. Launch copy: story "bits" for emails and live sales events (04b-main ~04:25–04:57)

Eileen Wilder (a launch speaker) calls a live sales event a **"money party"**, run by you or by "your creator or partner", and gave
three scripts ("bits", short stories) that "you could use… with your creators, with your partners… And you can also just write them
out in an email… The bits, the stories make money." She "first used these stories… in email" (04b-main ~04:10–04:40).
- **Structure of a bit:** a short story → "what's the takeaway for you" → an audience action (type a word in the chat) → the offer.
- **Bit 1, Gideon (identity):** a hiding man is called "mighty warrior"; "whatever follows the words 'I am' is directing your
  destiny"; list the audience's excuses ("too young… no audience… no money… not techie… camera shy… no expertise… no time") and
  answer each ("AI is the cheapest hire you're ever going to make… AI will work the night shift"); "the excuses are gone… only the
  hiding" → "get up" → the offer (~04:39–04:48).
- **Bit 2, law of advancement (reframing setbacks):** "before anything goes up, it has to go down" (seed, plane flaps, an eagle
  learning to fly by falling); "sometimes our bank balances are going down before our income goes up… That's not failure" (~04:48–04:52).
- **Bit 3, "your biggest expense is not knowing" (cost of inaction):** write $100,000, subtract last year's income: "this is what
  you paid to life for not knowing how to make a hundred thousand dollars"; then $1M − $100k; "how much longer do you want to keep
  paying life for not knowing?" → "You can write this in your emails and share this on your stories" (~04:52–04:57).
- **Price objections at the event:** see [buyer-psychology.md](buyer-psychology.md) §5b.

> Our note: all three bits were performed on the audience as the close for Monetize (with the join URL), so they are a catalogue
> of persuasion devices as much as copy templates. Bit 3 treats an imagined income as a real loss; we would not use it as is. No
> launch schedule, email count, pre-launch or conversion data was given.

## 4b. Saturation and local markets (04a-vip ~00:07–00:23)

- **Saturation, as answered:** most people "aren't going to apply" (MOTIVATION, ~00:04), and "the market is just so big". But one niche can be flooded: "If we told you, hey, just go to fitness micro creators and help them launch digital products, or you only launch a faceless fitness product, then yeah, you could be flooding the market. But there's just so many topics, there's so many creators, there's so many languages as well" (~00:08).
- **Local and less advanced markets as the edge:** "One of the biggest competitive advantages you can have is take everything you've learned here and go apply it to South Africa, go apply it to India, go apply it to Azerbaijan… countries where they're not as advanced in their marketing frameworks" (~00:09). David and Roman launched in Kazakhstan "not familiar with the language" (Ali, ~00:09).
- **Your language is a competitive advantage:** "How many operators do you know? How many people in the digital product space do you know in your country or in your language? So I always recommend go to these smaller markets" (~00:20).
- **Smaller ceilings, modest first targets:** "Maybe the Mongolian market… you're not going to make $10 million a year, but does it matter?… $2,500 a month first or $8,000 a month first" (~00:22).
- **Example:** Nader (Gaza) "created digital products in Arabic and just serviced that market… got to, I think, 10,000 USD" (~00:22).
- **Why smaller markets lag (Episode 4 main):** "Generally what happens is the American market gets hit first… whether that be software or physical products or digital products… The most advanced stuff goes to those markets first and they bleed into the smaller markets"; in them "maybe you might not hit $50 million, $100 million, but who cares… We're just trying to make an extra 2 grand a month, 5 grand a month, 10 grand a month" (04b-main ~03:06–03:08). "Less sophisticated", not "less developed" (Iman corrects himself).

## 5. Earlier principles (Episodes 1–4 VIP)

- **Buyers don't search for products; products are placed in front of them.** "Nobody wakes up and googles 'buy golf ebook'." Purchases happen "in a place that you were already spending time" and through "someone that you already trusted" (01-opp ~02:20).
- **Buyers are already gathered** "in groups of **10,000, 50,000, 100,000 people**… put your digital product in front of them" = **"untapped digital gold mines"** (01-opp ~02:21). Episode 3 names them as micro-creators' audiences (§2).
- **Cost and starting point:** "zero dollars", even with zero followers (01-opp ~02:21); in practice paid as a revenue share (§3.4).
- **Sales curve:** "At first, a few… once your distribution dials in, these numbers can scale" (01-opp ~02:22).
- **Product comes first:** 50,000 ready buyers + wrong product = $0 (02b-main ~01:08). See the tension in §7.
- **Specific products are easier to place** (02a-vip ~00:18).
- **Getting bought = standing out** (03a-vip ~00:28–00:29): (1) the right problem, actually fixed; (2) a trustworthy, premium look; (3) AI does ~90% of design. The "hidden method" teased in the VIP (03a-vip ~00:30) is most likely the **creator-personalised product** ("a hidden feature" in Synthesize, 03b-main ~01:48).
- **No face, no following needed** (03a-vip ~00:13); in the partner model the creator is the face (03b-main ~01:22). Behind the scenes is also safer: creator careers are "shorter than athletes… I would feel a lot of anxiety if all of your money is just made based on your face… being behind the scenes is actually probably one of the biggest advantages" (04a-vip ~00:18).
- **Worldwide and multilingual** (03a-vip ~00:42–00:44; 03b-main ~03:08).

## 7. Tensions

- **Search vs placement:** "Nobody… googles 'buy golf ebook'" (01-opp ~02:20) **vs** pockets chosen by what people search for on Google and Amazon (02b-main ~01:19, ~01:33) and the VIP presenter buying a wedding tracker via Google (03a-vip ~00:07).
- **Intrinsic vs borrowed value:** "the whole industry basically runs on intrinsic value" (03a-vip ~00:10) **vs** "virtually every single person… used" creators' audiences, and a product in the creator's branding is what sells (03b-main ~01:16–01:22).
- **Product vs creator:** the wrong product sells nothing in front of 50,000 buyers (02b-main ~01:08) **vs** "the better the creator, the worse you can be at everything else and still make money" (03b-main ~01:32) and "you can do pretty much everything wrong" with a trusting audience (~01:23).
- **$0 distribution vs a 50% split** (01-opp ~02:21 vs 03b-main ~01:39, ~01:46).
- **Creator size:** 10k–100k, under 10k "too small" (03b-main ~01:36) and big creators unreachable (~01:24) **vs** David's first partner with 2–3k followers ($3k) (03b-main ~02:51) and Roman's cold message to a 5M creator ($1.7M, PROOF, ~03:00).
- **Filter 3 vs the psychologist case:** skip creators selling "course, ebook, or coaching" (03b-main ~01:36) **vs** the $600k case was a creator already selling 1:1 sessions (03b-main ~03:02).

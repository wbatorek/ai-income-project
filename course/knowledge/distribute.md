## 1. Distribution, not selling (01-opp ~02:14–02:16)

- "Forget the word selling… you don't need to sell to be successful. What you need instead is something called **distribution**" (01-opp ~02:15).
- **Supermarket analogy:** cereal brands never cold-call you. They "position their products in a place where most people go. Where people are already predisposed to buy what they sell… They're just putting themselves in front of **presold buyers**" (01-opp ~02:15).
- Done right, "you don't need to sell, but you still end up selling thousands of times more than the other people who are focused on sales" (01-opp ~02:15).

## 2. What has been taught about the method

- **Buyers don't search for products; products are placed in front of them.** "Nobody wakes up and googles 'buy golf ebook'." Purchases happen "in a place that you were already spending time" and through "someone that you already trusted" (01-opp ~02:20).
- **Buyers are already gathered.** "The buyers for virtually any product are already gathered somewhere online in groups of **10,000, 50,000, 100,000 people**. And all you have to do is put your digital product in front of them." These gathering places are **"untapped digital gold mines"** (01-opp ~02:21).
- **Untapped markets** where "potential buyers are already actively looking for what they sell" (01-opp ~02:20).
- **Cost and starting point:** access "anywhere from 10,000 to 100,000 potential buyers and do it for **zero dollars**", even with **zero followers** (01-opp ~02:21).
- **Sales curve:** "At first, a few. One here, two there. But once your distribution dials in, these numbers can scale a lot. And they can scale fast" (01-opp ~02:22).
- **Product comes first:** the right audience can't save the wrong product; 50,000 ready buyers + wrong product = $0 (02b-main ~01:08).
- **Specific products are easier to place:** "we're going to show you in episode three how to put these products in front of people who are already looking to buy them… even if they look very, very specific, that's actually a good thing" (02a-vip ~00:18).
- **Getting bought = standing out** (03a-vip ~00:28–00:29):
  1. **The right problem, actually fixed.** "If you're fixing the right problem, you automatically start to stand out… Taking someone from point A to point B is the number one thing."
  2. **How it looks.** "Often a sale is made based on trust… the way that your digital product is represented and looks is a part of that." A premium product "deserves premium branding".
  3. **Let AI do the design:** "AI should be doing 90% of the work when it comes to design, especially in the early stages, and you should just be checking it."
- **No face, no following needed** for intrinsic-value products: the objections "I don't want to show my face. I don't have a following. My English is bad… I don't live in the US" are "completely irrelevant" (03a-vip ~00:13).
- **The market is worldwide and multilingual.** Michal's buyers came from New Zealand, the US, Dubai and India; you "can sell in whatever language… you want" and "create everything with AI in whatever language" (Spanish; a seller in the Lithuanian market) (03a-vip ~00:42–00:44).

## 3. Examples seen so far

- **Own faceless channel (Michal):** didn't want to be on camera or script videos, so he made motivational-quote reels with **ChatGPT + Canva** and posted **every day** on Instagram; this page became the audience for his first ebook (Instagram growth) (02b-main ~03:38, ~03:42). "It's not like you start working today and you get results tomorrow" (02b-main ~03:38).
  - Format detail: **black background, white text**, an idea ChatGPT suggested; started 18 July 2025; posted **every day for three months** before anything happened; then one reel "went around 7 million, 13 million" views and brought followers (03a-vip ~00:40).
  - **The audience revealed the demand:** followers messaged "how are you growing your page?", so "when I saw the demand, that's when I felt like creating a digital product… I created a product which I knew about" (03a-vip ~00:49).
  - Still faceless on the profile (no picture) while selling (03a-vip ~00:41).
- **Product made for a creator's audience (Michal):** a follower who is a creator "needed an ADHD ebook"; he built it with Synthesize, edited it to her requests and was paid **~$70** (02b-main ~03:44). In the Day 3 VIP: **$67**, and he then **offered done-for-you ebooks in his Instagram stories** ("who needs ebooks or digital products, just DM me"), which made "my first few thousand dollars" (03a-vip ~00:46).
- **Daily simple content → audience → products (Visualize Value):** Jack Butcher posted "very simple black and white graphics, like one a day" turning an idea into a picture; later sold courses and a one-page planner; $58 in the account in January 2020, over $1M in sales by year-end, "without spending any money on acquisition, any money on ads" (03a-vip ~00:12).
- **Marketplace best seller (Avada):** the best-selling website template on ThemeForest; 800k sales, $60M; "no one knew who these people were" (03a-vip ~00:12).
- **Pre-sales (Josh Comeau):** opened pre-sales for an unfinished course; 4,800 pre-orders at ~$120, $577k in a week (03a-vip ~00:16).

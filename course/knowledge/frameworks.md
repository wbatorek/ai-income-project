## 1. Own what AI builds (01-opp ~01:43–01:47)

- **Thesis:** "AI works for whoever owns the thing it's working on." Using AI at your job or for clients makes the company or the client richer, not you (01-opp ~01:44).
- **The test question:** "When AI finishes the work, whose is it? And if the answer isn't mine, well, then AI isn't working for you" (01-opp ~01:45).
- **Landlord logic:** "Owners don't get paid for what they know. They get paid for what they own" (01-opp ~01:46).
- **Assets, not time:** escape the rat race by collecting assets (Rich Dad Poor Dad). Assets used to need capital; AI digital products are assets "that cost you virtually nothing to build" (01-opp ~01:46).
- **AI digital products** = the course's chosen asset: "Plug AI into digital products, and you get the first asset in history with no price of admission" (01-opp ~01:49).

## 2. Digital real estate

- **Build once, paid many times:** "Just like the property that is built once and pays you an infinite number of times. Here, we have a PDF that was created once and can be sold an infinite number of times" (01-opp ~01:49).
- **Portfolio view:** each new product is "a new rental property", except it costs nothing to build (label from a MOTIVATION segment, 01-opp ~01:55; recapped as "digital real estate: build once, get paid over and over", 02b-main ~01:05).
- **Recipe, not cake:** the same idea from the VIP call. Create the recipe once, sell it without limit; a cake must be baked every time (02a-vip ~00:11).

## 3. The 3D system: Develop → Distribute → Deliver (01-opp ~02:16–02:25)

- **Origin (claimed):** analysis of every AI digital product that made over $100,000 on their leaderboard; all share three elements (01-opp ~02:17).
- **Universal:** every product (energy drink, chair, computer) is developed, distributed and delivered; here it's all digital (01-opp ~02:17).

| Step | Core idea | Detail |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Develop** | "Nothing happens until something is bought." AI removes the build barrier (01-opp ~02:19) | [develop.md](develop.md), [choosing-products.md](choosing-products.md) |
| **2. Distribute** | Put the product in front of presold buyers already gathered in groups of 10k–100k (01-opp ~02:21): in practice **micro-creators' audiences**, on revenue share (03b-main ~01:19–01:30) | [distribute.md](distribute.md) |
| **3. Deliver** | Automate so 10 or 10,000 sales take the same work (01-opp ~02:24); the platform also pays the partner's share (03b-main ~01:55) | [deliver.md](deliver.md) |

"Everything that we're going to be talking about over the next few days will be built on top of this three-step process" (01-opp ~02:25).

## 4. The stop test (01-opp ~02:18)

- A job is "one-dimensional… hours in, money out. **That dies the moment that you stop.**" A real asset doesn't (01-opp ~02:18).
- **Test:** "If I stop, does the money stop?" Yes = a job or a second job; no = an asset.
- Same idea: delivery that runs "while you're at work, asleep, or on a beach" is why "this side hustle never turns into a second job" (01-opp ~02:24).

> Our note: the short "If I stop, does the money stop?" wording is our paraphrase of 01-opp ~02:18 (see the note under segment 24 of the Episode 1 transcript).

- **Tension:** the Day 3 VIP presents coaching as something you can bundle or even make "your full digital product" (03a-vip ~00:27), and its case study's best offer is an ebook + **1:1 mentorship** at $1,500 (03a-vip ~00:38). 1:1 time fails the stop test.

## 5. Turning Point

- **Definition A:** the point of being able to afford *not* to work: leave a job or a place, spend days with family (PROOF segment, 01-opp ~01:52).
- **Definition B:** "the day side income passes main income" (MOTIVATION segment, 02b-main ~01:49).

> Our note: the Turning Point is used as a goal, not taught as a method, and appears only in PROOF/MOTIVATION segments. The two definitions differ: B (side income > main income) is a measurable milestone; A (freedom not to work) is a later, vaguer state. Our session notes for Episode 1 use B.

## 6. Distribution, not selling (01-opp ~02:14–02:16)

Supermarket model: be where presold buyers already are, instead of pitching. See [distribute.md](distribute.md).

**Getting bought = standing out** (03a-vip ~00:28–00:29): (1) the right problem, actually fixed (A → B is "the number one thing"); (2) a trustworthy, premium look, because "a sale is made based on trust"; (3) AI does ~90% of the design, a human checks. A further "hidden method" was deferred to the main session.

## 6b. Intrinsic vs borrowed value (03a-vip ~00:06–00:14)

- **Intrinsic value:** the value "lies in the thing itself, not in the person that's endorsing it or the person that created it" (Google Maps; a wedding tracker) (~00:09, ~00:11).
- **Borrowed value:** value lent by the creator's fame (the signed Ronaldo jersey); allows a higher price; "the absolute minority" of the market (~00:09–00:10).
- **The right question:** "Does it take someone from point A to point B? Does it fix the problem? If it does that, then it doesn't matter who you are" (~00:08).
- **Use:** intrinsic value is what makes faceless selling possible: "the product is completely disconnected from who you are" (~00:13). Borrowed value is to be taught in the main session (~00:10).
- **Borrowed value in practice (03b-main):** a faceless seller borrows a creator's trust: the product carries the creator's name, voice and branding, and the creator recommends it (~01:16–01:23, ~01:47–01:48). The main session presents this as how "virtually every single person" in its case studies earned (~01:22), which contradicts "the industry runs on intrinsic value" (see the [README](README.md) table).

## 6c. Distribution frameworks (03b-main)

- **Hope marketing vs rinse and repeat:** random trend-chasing gives "feast or famine income"; a repeatable placement in front of predisposed buyers does not (~01:12–01:13).
- **Supply, not demand:** "demand is never your problem. Supply is"; a gold mine = proven distribution with an empty shelf (~01:15).
- **Plain vs branded jersey:** a product in a creator's branding sells to their fans the way a club jersey does (~01:16–01:19, ~01:47).
- **Stadiums without jerseys:** creators have audiences and no product; "a shortage of products for the buyers to buy" (~01:20).
- **Micro-creators:** 10k–100k followers, still personally connected to their audience (~01:25–01:26).
- **The three switches:** partner → personalize → publish (~01:31). [distribute.md](distribute.md) §3.
- **The four filters:** video views 50k–200k; followers 10k–100k; no own product; business email in bio (~01:34–01:38). [distribute.md](distribute.md) §3.1.
- **0 → 5 beats 5 → 8:** pick creators who earn nothing from their audience yet (~01:37).
- **Partnership maths:** price × followers × conversion × your share (~01:38–01:40).
- **One creator, then copy-paste the launch** (guests, ~03:05).
- **Mark Tilbury's three lessons:** (1) get to the front of an existing queue, "and for the first time, the front of the queue is free"; (2) "the new door has no competition yet" (early channels); (3) "the person with the audience has the queue, but nothing on the shelf" (~03:16–03:44). His question: "Who already has the customers? And what could I create that they'd be willing to pay for?" (~03:43).

## 7. Product-choice frameworks

- **Five digital product unlocks:** pain → worsening pain → transformation → specificity → speed (02a-vip ~00:50). [choosing-products.md](choosing-products.md) §3.
- **Four-part product promise:** who + worsening problem + result + time (02a-vip ~00:51). [choosing-products.md](choosing-products.md) §4.
- **Point A → point B map:** A = feel, fear, avoid, self-image; map A first (02a-vip ~00:38).
- **Profitable pocket:** specific person × specific problem that big companies ignore (02b-main ~01:30–01:31).
- **3-part demand test:** already searched for + already paid for + no good solution yet (02b-main ~01:33).
- **Passion vs demand:** "One of them starts with you. The other starts with a buyer" (snowboards in the Bahamas) (02b-main ~01:27).
- **First idea = worst idea:** push past the obvious (transportation exercise) (02b-main ~01:29).
- **Ideas are almost worthless:** go for proven and simple (02a-vip ~00:23).
- **Pain is 90%** (02a-vip ~00:27).
- **Intrinsic vs borrowed value; "does it take someone from A to B?"** (03a-vip ~00:08–00:10). §6b.
- **Creator first:** in the partner model the pocket is found through a creator ("industry + keyword" search) and the product idea comes from the creator's content (03b-main ~01:34, ~01:48).

## 8. Build frameworks

- **The three secrets of Develop:** pocket → synthesise → package (02b-main ~01:15).
- **Synthesis over expertise + consensus positioning:** 150+ sources; "Here is what the experts agree on" (02b-main ~01:36).
- **Minimum viable digital product:** ~45-page PDF to "test if it works or not" (02b-main ~01:41).
- **Find the gold first, then keep digging:** validate with the simplest format, upgrade on success (02b-main ~01:45).
- **Start simple, add complexity once simple works:** ebook → course → digital mind (02a-vip ~00:15).
- **Value ≠ volume:** "Nobody pays for volume… They pay to make a problem go away" (02b-main ~01:41); 20 pages can beat 200 (02a-vip ~00:20).
- **Iteration ladder / portfolio:** $0, $0, $0 → $15/day → $50/day → $100/day ≈ $5k/month (02b-main ~01:48).
- **Faceless by format:** ebook and asset as they are; video via Loom or Slides + voiceover, not avatars; digital mind via no-code platforms (03a-vip ~00:14–00:23).
- **AI does 90% of design, you check** (03a-vip ~00:29).
- **Pre-sell when the value is there** (Josh Comeau, 4,800 pre-orders) (03a-vip ~00:17).

## 9. Small numbers

- 37 subscribers × $250/month ≈ $9k/month (01-opp ~01:55; 02a-vip ~00:18).
- "You don't need millions of people. You need a few hundred people with the same specific problem" (02b-main ~01:30).

> Our note: the "small-wins ladder" ($100 → $1,000 → higher) and "move now, perfect later / product iteration" came from PROOF and MOTIVATION segments (02b-main ~03:30, ~04:05) and are not counted as course knowledge. They agree with §8's iteration ladder.

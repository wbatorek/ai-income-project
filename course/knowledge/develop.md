## 1. The premise

- **"Nothing happens until something is bought."** You need a product, and building one was historically "the highest barrier to entry"; AI collapsed it. You can develop a product "with zero knowledge, zero expertise, and zero authority… We use AI to create the product for you" (01-opp ~02:19).
- **Woodworking demo:** a woodworking product "put together without knowing a single thing about woodworking in a few minutes with AI" (shown on slides only) (01-opp ~02:19).
- **No expertise, no skill, no capital, no face:** the method works "without being an expert at anything… without spending a dollar to start, and without ever showing your face" (02b-main ~01:08).
- **Whose output is it?** Use AI to build something *you* own, not work for an employer or client (01-opp ~01:45). See [frameworks.md](frameworks.md).

## 2. The three secrets of Develop (02b-main ~01:15)

1. **Identify the right topic**: a profitable pocket ([choosing-products.md](choosing-products.md) §5).
2. **Synthesise all the knowledge that already exists** on that topic online.
3. **Package it** into a finished product you own.

"What used to be the hardest part of this process, actually developing a product, has now become the easiest" (02b-main ~01:15).

## 3. Secret 2: synthesise 150+ sources (02b-main ~01:34–01:37)

- **You don't create the solution; it already exists**, "scattered across scientific studies, research papers, business reports, courses, articles, videos… You're never inventing anything here. All you're doing is simply assembling what already works" (02b-main ~01:35).
- **One source is not enough:** "it's not as simple as reading one study and calling it a solution… That would be a poor and incomplete solution. And that is why we use **at least 150 references**. That is what separates a digital product that actually delivers on its promise from the recycled garbage" (02b-main ~01:36).
- **Manual version is impractical:** 150 studies × 20–100 pages, summarised "into 40 pages", on a topic you don't know: "months of work" (02b-main ~01:36).
- **Output:** "one simple step-by-step solution to the problem that you identified", which becomes "the foundation that your entire digital product gets built on" (02b-main ~01:36).
- **Positioning (replaces expertise):** never claim "I'm an expert". Say "Here is what the experts on this topic agree on after analyzing over 150 scientific studies." No face, no pretending (02b-main ~01:36).
- **Include the references** so the product is "backed by real research. And you can point to every single source" (02b-main ~01:36).

**Synthesis checklist (as taught):**
- [ ] Topic = a validated pocket with a specific problem
- [ ] ≥ 150 sources: studies, papers, business reports, courses, articles, videos
- [ ] Merged into one step-by-step method that solves that one problem
- [ ] Framed as expert consensus, not personal authority
- [ ] Reference list included

## 4. Secret 3: package as a minimum viable product (02b-main ~01:40–01:42)

- **Bigger isn't more valuable.** The common mistake: "spend 3 months building the big product just to figure out that it doesn't work any better than something that they could have finished this week" (02b-main ~01:41).
- **Format:** "instead of packaging our digital product as a 5-hour-long course… we package it as a simple **45-page PDF** that solves the exact problem that your profitable pocket is built around. This is your **minimum viable digital product**" (02b-main ~01:41).
- **Purpose:** "something that you can get out there fast, with minimal amounts of work, just to **test if it works or not**. No writing, no editing, no website to build" (02b-main ~01:41).
- **Time:** "around 40 minutes… with the right specialized AI" (02b-main ~01:41); the whole process "in less than an hour" (02b-main ~01:15).
- **Proof that PDFs sell:** Woodworking Guide and "No Grid Survival Project", "both of which made over 7 figures". "45 pages that solves a problem someone is already up at night searching for… is worth far more than 12 hours of video that they're never going to open" (02b-main ~01:42).
- **Start simple, add complexity later:** ebook or digital asset first; turn a working ebook into a course, then a digital mind (02a-vip ~00:15). Demo: "We're just trying to create the MVP here… if you find gold, you can keep digging. But let's find the gold first" (02b-main ~01:45).
- **Value = transformation and speed, not length:** a 20-page ebook can beat a 200-page one (02a-vip ~00:20, ~00:49); a 4-week plan "could be five pages" (02a-vip ~00:40).

### 5.1 Real use (EXAMPLE, Michal) (02b-main ~03:42–03:47)

- **With ChatGPT:** his first ebook ("Instagram from 0 to 1,000 followers under 45 days") took **almost two weeks**, 3–4 h a day, prompting and placing images by hand (~03:42).
- **With Synthesize:** input "I need an ebook for ADHD" → **15–20 topics with scores** (8.9, 9.7) → picked the highest → client asked for changes → typed edits manually, images already in → finished in **2–4 days** (~03:44).
- He found specialised tools better than "the normal ones like Claude, ChatGPT. They are good though. For daily basis" (~03:47).

## 6. Iterate and build a portfolio (02b-main ~01:48)

- **You're not stuck with the first product.** "You don't like it?… You create another one. It's not making money. You create another one… until you publish one that makes you money" (~01:48).
- **Expect early zeros:** products 1, 2 and 3 make $0; maybe the 5th or 10th makes **$15/day**; keep going to one at **$50/day**, then **$100/day** (~01:48).
- **Portfolio math:** $15 + $50 + $100 a day ≈ **$5,000/month** from 3 products (~01:48).
- **Each new product is another asset** in the portfolio (01-opp ~01:49).

**Iteration loop (as taught):** pick pocket → build MVP → publish → no money? build the next one → keep the earners → when one works, "keep digging" (more formats) (02b-main ~01:45, ~01:48; 02a-vip ~00:15).

## 7. Contradictions

- **Page count:** 1 or 20 pages doesn't matter (01-opp ~01:48); 20 or even 5 pages can beat 200 (02a-vip ~00:20, ~00:40) **vs** a ~45-page PDF as the standard (02b-main ~01:41) and "summarize it into 40 pages" (02b-main ~01:36).
- **Build time:** "in minutes" (01-opp ~02:19), "around 40 minutes" (02b-main ~01:41), "less than an hour" (02b-main ~01:15), 5–10 min of generation (02b-main ~01:46) **vs** Michal's 2–4 days with manual edits (02b-main ~03:44).
- **"No writing, no editing"** (02b-main ~01:41) **vs** Michal typing changes by hand (02b-main ~03:44).
- **Solution quality:** "it doesn't really matter what solution you propose" (02a-vip ~00:39) **vs** 150 references are what separate a product that "delivers on its promise" from "recycled garbage" (02b-main ~01:36).

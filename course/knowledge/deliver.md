## 1. Why delivery matters

- **Fast growth breaks manual delivery.** "One of the number one reasons that people fail in this digital product industry is that they grow too fast. And their delivery breaks" (01-opp ~02:23).
- Sales can go "from a few hundred dollars to tens of thousands of dollars in a matter of a few weeks", so delivery must be in place first (01-opp ~02:23).

## 2. What has been taught

- **Automate it completely.** "The right system can deliver your product automatically with zero input from you. So fulfilling 10 sales or 10,000 sales becomes virtually the same" (01-opp ~02:24); "the work to deliver 10 units or 10,000 units of your product becomes virtually the same" (03b-main ~01:08).
- **In a partnership, delivery includes paying the partner:** the product must be delivered "automatically without you or the creator lifting a finger", and each side paid its share (03b-main ~01:54–01:55).
- **Same work at any volume:** "you can make 10 sales or 10,000 sales, and the work is pretty much the same… you might need to answer a few more emails" (02a-vip ~00:12).
- **Access is granted automatically** on the platforms the course uses: "you create once, and then you just give people access. And that happens automatically" (02a-vip ~00:12).
- **One-person business:** "You don't need to hire a bloated team or any employees" (01-opp ~02:24).
- **It runs while you're away:** a sale "while you're at work, asleep, or on a beach… gets delivered automatically" (01-opp ~02:24).
- **No second job:** "You create the product once, and after that, every sale delivers itself" (01-opp ~02:24).
- **Scaling has none of the usual constraints:** no logistics, suppliers or storage (01-opp ~02:24).
- **No manual sending:** Iman once delivered his first ebook by email; now "they pay, they get access… and it's basically a done deal" (03a-vip ~00:25).
- **A simple payment process sells more:** "People underestimate how important a simple payment process actually is when you're trying to make money with digital products. It makes a huge, huge difference on the amount of sales that you make" (03a-vip ~00:25).
- **Payment gateways are a real blocker outside the US/UK:** the presenter (South Africa) had no Stripe access when starting, "one of the biggest blocks" (03a-vip ~00:24).
- **Platform risk (EXAMPLE):** Michal's PayPal account "was good for a week", then frozen and "permanently banned", with money stuck; he moved to Whop (03a-vip ~00:36).

## 3. Whop

- Presented as "the best way to publish a digital product because it's quick and it's free to do so… And it takes care of your entire delivery system" (01-opp ~02:24).
- Examples sold on Whop: Pokémon cards (€25/month) and Fortnite maps ($250/month), both subscriptions (01-opp ~01:55).
- The step-by-step Whop setup was promised for Episode 3 (01-opp ~02:24) and shown as a publish flow from Synthesize (§3.3).

### 3.1 What the platform does (03a-vip ~00:24–00:28; presented by a co-owner, "a little bit biased")

| Function | As described |
|---|---|
| Store page | AI builds the landing page; "no need to like worry about building websites"; a professional-looking page "in like 10 minutes" (~00:24) |
| Formats | Ebooks, video programs, digital minds, templates, in one or several "communities" (~00:24) |
| Payments | One-time charge, subscription, instalments (~00:25) |
| Delivery | Access granted on payment (~00:25) |
| Dashboard | Track all sales at all times (~00:25) |
| Reach | "241 countries" supported (~00:26) |
| Cost | "Free to start"; no charge per product created, so you can make "as many products as you want" (PITCH, ~00:26) |
| Add-ons | Communities, live calls and chats, coaching ("the coaching could be your full digital product"), files and content pages via apps, **iFrame** to embed custom tools such as a digital mind, an app store (~00:27) |

- The company runs all its own communities and coaching on Whop (03a-vip ~00:27).
- Add-ons are for going beyond a "very basic digital product where it just solves A to B", e.g. letting buyers talk to each other (03a-vip ~00:27).

### 3.2 Whop in a creator partnership (03b-main ~01:54–01:56; presented by a co-owner)

- "Specifically built for people offering digital products, ebooks, PDFs, courses, memberships, coaching programs" (~01:55).
- **Three jobs:** "It processes the payments. It delivers the digital product automatically. It sends your cut of the revenue directly to your bank account and whatever cut you agree with the creator to theirs. So that way you never have to pay any creator out of pocket" (~01:55).
- **Set up once:** "you can just set this up once and make as much money as you want from this creator's digital product without having to touch it again" (~01:55).
- **One place, no tech stack;** "especially helpful if you live in a country where it's hard to get payments online" (Cape Verde example) (~01:55).
- Claimed scale: "our users have earned over $6 billion in payouts" (~01:55).

### 3.3 Publishing steps (recorded demo, inside Synthesize, 03b-main ~01:56–01:59)

| # | Step | What was said |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Finished product → **Store page details** | "We don't even need to leave Synthesize… our own store page here within the software connected directly to Whop" |
| 2 | **Generate store page content** | The copy is written from the product "curated for this specific partnership and influencer" |
| 3 | **Connect** the store page to Whop | One button |
| 4 | **Set the price** | "$45" in the demo; then a toggle (not explained) |
| 5 | **Generate icon**, **store image**, **store cover** | AI-generated art |
| 6 | **Publish to Whop** → Publish | "Your store is live now" |
| 7 | Done → **View your store** | Whop admin dashboard |
| 8 | **Copy the link** and **send it to the creator** | "When someone presses buy now, you will get paid. The creator will get paid" |

**Generic version:** product → sales-page copy → price → cover/thumbnail → publish on a checkout platform → share a link with the partner.

### 3.4 Prices and fees mentioned (03b-main)

- **One-time** price only in the demo: **$45** (~01:57). The VIP listed one-time, subscription and instalments (03a-vip ~00:25).
- "$150… on the low end of what these personalized digital products actually sell for" (EXAMPLE, ~01:38).
- **No fees** were mentioned, and the split configuration (does the creator need a Whop account?) was not shown.

## 4. Contradictions

- **Price level:** "$150" is "the low end" for personalised products (03b-main ~01:38) **vs** $45 in the publishing demo (03b-main ~01:57).
- **"Free":** Whop is "free to do so" (01-opp ~02:24), the platforms are "completely free to use" (02a-vip ~00:12) and "free to start" with no charge per product (03a-vip ~00:26) **vs** the host's own disclosure that **Whop takes a small % of every transaction**: "the more you earn, the more Whop earns" (PITCH, 01-opp ~01:24).

> Our note: the host co-owns Whop, so the Whop recommendation is not neutral; compare fees and features with Gumroad, Etsy, Payhip, Lemon Squeezy etc. in [`../../research/`](../../research/) before committing. The Monetize pitch also listed "automated payments, delivery and customer service, 24/7" as part of its Deliver training (PITCH, 02b-main ~02:03); customer service was never taught. In the partner model, Whop's split also needs comparing with simpler options, such as the creator promoting through a high-commission affiliate link.

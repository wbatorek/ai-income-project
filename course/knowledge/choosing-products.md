## 1. Why this step matters most

- **The product is what everything hangs on.** "You could put a product in front of 50,000 people who are ready to spend money, but if it's the wrong product, you'd make nothing" (02b-main ~01:08).
- **The topic decides earnings, not effort:** "The reason that they all earned that much isn't how hard they worked, it's the topic that they picked" (02b-main ~01:14).
- **Core reduction:** "finding someone with a problem that they are willing to pay to solve and creating a product for them. That's all we're doing here" (02b-main ~01:19).
- **Pain is "90% of the way there":** "If we find a painful problem to solve and a good solution for it… you can really get everything else wrong… and you're still going to make money" (02a-vip ~00:27, ~00:32).
- **Picking the right pocket is "basically half the way"** to a successful product (02b-main ~01:33).
- **The right problem is also how you stand out:** "If you're fixing the right problem, you automatically start to stand out… Taking someone from point A to point B is the number one thing that is actually going to help you stand out" (03a-vip ~00:28).

## 2. Idea rules: before you choose

- **An idea is almost worthless.** Don't look for a "new telephone" or an Uber-style invention. "Most of the times, you want a very simple idea that people already think about… It's so much easier to go for something that's proven and simple" (02a-vip ~00:23).
- **Ask the right question.** Not "who am I to sell this? Nobody knows me", but "does it take someone from point A to point B? Does it fix the problem? If it does that, then it doesn't matter who you are" (03a-vip ~00:08).
- **It just needs to fix a problem.** "As long as human beings have problems, the demand for digital products is always going to be there" (02a-vip ~00:23). "If your product doesn't fix any pain point, no one's going to buy it" (02a-vip ~00:22).
- **The passion trap.** "The market doesn't care about your passion. Not even a little." It only cares whether your product "solves a problem that people are already desperately looking for an answer to and can't find a good enough solution to yet" (02b-main ~01:26).
  - Snowboard shop on a beach in the Bahamas: not a bad topic, wrong buyers. "One of them starts with you. The other starts with a buyer" (02b-main ~01:27).
  - Typical failure: weeks of work on a topic you love, a genuinely good product, $0, because "they just picked a topic with no buyers in it" (02b-main ~01:26).
- **Your first ideas are your worst ideas.** Transportation exercise: car, bus, plane come first; jet packs and Iron Man suits come from people "who kept going when their brains wanted to stop… that's actually where the good ideas live" (02b-main ~01:29).
- **Let the AI decide.** Humans pick first ideas and have preferences; the AI "looks at what people are already searching for, already paying for, and not finding a good enough solution to" and hands you **20 pockets scored by profit potential**. "Don't know which one to pick? Just go with the highest" (02b-main ~01:33).

## 3. The five digital product unlocks

Order: **pain → worsening pain → transformation → specificity → speed** (02a-vip ~00:50). The course's AI scores every idea on exactly these five: "If you can have one that scores high in every single one of those criteria, you have a very, very good digital product" (02a-vip ~00:50).

### Unlock 1: Pain (02a-vip ~00:21–00:29)

- **Definition:** the pain point the product removes. "The most important question that you need to ask yourself" (02a-vip ~00:22).
- **Pain beats pleasure.** People "move away from pain two times more intensively than they move towards pleasure" (02a-vip ~00:25). Pleasure problems (new phone, PS5, better photos) are "not the best types of problems for us to pick" (02a-vip ~00:25).
- **Three pillars** where pains live: **wealth, health, relationships**. Hobbies are a weaker fourth pillar because they are usually pleasure (02a-vip ~00:26).
- **Measure: reminder frequency.** "The more often you're reminded of that problem… the more painful that is" (02a-vip ~00:28). Toothache: every time you eat, talk, work, sleep. Teeth whitening: once or twice a week (02a-vip ~00:27–00:28).
- **Weak vs strong examples:**

| Weak | Strong |
|---|---|
| How to cut a watermelon: nobody has the problem, nobody would pay (02a-vip ~00:22) | Toothache at 2 a.m.: fixed "in two, three days, I guarantee you" (02a-vip ~00:27) |
| Teeth whitening before summer photos: "a little bit of pain" (02a-vip ~00:27) | |
| Better photography: a hobby, low pain (02a-vip ~00:26) | |
| General weight loss: reminded only "a few times a week", people "got used to it" (02a-vip ~00:28) | |

### Unlock 2: Worsening pain (02a-vip ~00:29–00:33)

- **Definition:** how much worse the pain gets over time, and how fast. "The worse it gets and the faster it gets worse, the more desirable the solution" (02a-vip ~00:30).
- **Urgency** is the point where "you just got to fix that problem". Toothache stages: sensitive → dull ache → throbbing → can't eat or sleep → call the dentist (02a-vip ~00:31).
- **Flat vs rising:** overweight (worse over "years and years"), wanting white teeth (flat), windscreen chip (flat) **vs** toothache (daily), faulty car battery that strands you 40 min from home: "I'm buying a new battery **today**" (02a-vip ~00:30–00:32).
- **Mostly follows from choosing the right pain**, but still use it as a separate filter (02a-vip ~00:31).
- **Sign of worsening in a description:** the person has *started* avoiding something ("started sitting out games") (02a-vip ~00:38).

### Unlock 3: Transformation (02a-vip ~00:34–00:42)

- **Definition:** buyers pay to go from **point A** (current painful state) to **point B** (ideal end state), not for pages or megabytes. "They're buying something that you cannot see on screen" (02a-vip ~00:35).
- **Map point A first**, then B becomes obvious. Point A = **how they feel, what they fear, what they avoid, how they see themselves** (02a-vip ~00:38).
- **Every pain has its own transformation**, even inside one topic: in football, "shooting with more power and playing without knee pain are two completely different problems" with different buyers (02a-vip ~00:37). Each parent's ideal evening routine differs (02a-vip ~00:36).
- **Worked example (02a-vip ~00:38):**
  - A: knee hurts after every match; afraid of giving up football; started sitting out games; used to be on the main squad; thinks his playing days are over.
  - B: plays a full match without pain; back in the team; sees himself as a player again.
- See [buyer-psychology.md](buyer-psychology.md) for articulation and "why pay when info is free".

### Unlock 4: Specificity (02a-vip ~00:44–00:48; 02b-main ~01:30–01:31)

- **Definition:** a narrow, clearly identified person with a clearly identified problem. Big companies (Nike) must sell to the masses; small sellers can serve one type of person (02a-vip ~00:45; 02b-main ~01:30).
- **Narrowing ladder:** parents → dads → **divorced dads with teenage sons** (02a-vip ~00:45–00:46).
- **Boundary rule:** "specific enough that the right person feels it, but common enough that plenty of people have that problem" (02a-vip ~00:46). Don't narrow to one city.
- **Narrow by a distinguishing trait or problem, not an adjective that fits everyone.** "Busy moms dropping fat fast" is fake-specific: "every single mom is busy" (02a-vip ~00:47).
- **Good:** "New moms building repeatable newborn bedtimes without rigid schedules"; "Dual-working parents cooking complete dinners without chopping" (02a-vip ~00:47).

### Unlock 5: Speed (02a-vip ~00:48–00:50)

- **Definition:** for the same result, the faster route is worth more. 20 pages beats 200 pages when both solve it; 2 months beats 6 months (02a-vip ~00:49).
- **Dentist:** 5 minutes beats 5 hours. "What matters is, is the pain fixed or not?" (02a-vip ~00:49; repeated 02b-main ~01:41).
- **Caveat: must stay realistic.** "Lose 10 kilos in two days" isn't believable, "and then it doesn't matter" (02a-vip ~00:49).

## 4. The product promise (02a-vip ~00:51)

A sentence with four parts:
1. **Who:** a specific person
2. **Problem:** a painful problem, ideally getting worse
3. **Result:** point B, the transformation
4. **Time:** how long it takes

**Template:** "I help **[specific person]** with **[specific problem that's getting worse]** get **[point B]** in **[time]**."

**Example:** "I help first-time parents of 4 to 6 month olds whose nights get harder every week get their baby sleeping through the night in seven nights." (02a-vip ~00:51)

Same structure in Episode 2 main: "recover your pre-baby body in 12 weeks after giving birth" vs "weight loss" (02b-main ~01:31); Michal's "0 to 1,000 followers under 45 days" (02b-main ~03:42).

**Checklist for a promise:**
- [ ] Who is narrowed by a distinguishing trait (not "busy")
- [ ] Problem is a pain (wealth/health/relationships), felt often
- [ ] Worsening is written into the problem clause ("harder every week")
- [ ] Result is an observable end state
- [ ] Time frame is short and believable

## 5. The profitable pocket (02b-main ~01:14–01:34)

- **Definition:** "A profitable pocket is never a broad topic. It's always a **specific person with a specific problem**" (02b-main ~01:31).
- **Why it exists:** ideas "hidden in plain sight" that most people **and big companies** ignore, because big companies need millions of customers. "A pocket small enough that nobody big has bothered to serve it. And specific enough that the people inside of it will pay to have it solved" (02b-main ~01:30).
- **Size:** "You don't need millions of people. You need a few hundred people with the same specific problem" (02b-main ~01:30). "We massively underestimate how many people are sitting inside these pockets" (Jonathan: 240,000 buyers) (02b-main ~01:32). See §7 for the conflicting size figures.
- **There are thousands of them; you need one** (02b-main ~01:14).

### 5.1 The 3-part demand test

A pocket is worth building on when people are:
1. **already searching for** it,
2. **already paying for** it, and
3. **not finding a good enough solution** (the gap).

"It looks at what people are already searching for, already paying for, and not finding a good enough solution to… it identifies the exact gaps where a product can come in and win" (02b-main ~01:33). Same in other words: "a problem that people are already desperately looking for an answer to and can't find a good enough solution to yet" (02b-main ~01:26); "topics with hungry buyers, and almost no good solution yet" (02b-main ~01:19); "topics that people are already paying for" (02b-main ~01:08); "a problem that people are already paying to fix" (02b-main ~01:35).

**Where to look (named, not demonstrated):** "AI can scan **Google, Amazon, and all online communities** and find the gaps" (02b-main ~01:19).

**Where to look (EXAMPLE): your audience's questions.** Michal's followers kept messaging "how are you growing your page?"; "when I saw the demand, that's when I felt like creating a digital product… I created a product which I knew about" (03a-vip ~00:49). Later his followers asked "can I get this?" and he made ebooks on request (03a-vip ~00:46).

### 5.2 Broad topic → pocket (02b-main ~01:30–01:31)

| Broad topic | Profitable pocket |
|---|---|
| Diet / weight loss | Buckwheat diet for German women |
| Weight loss | Postpartum weight loss for new mothers ("pre-baby body in 12 weeks") |
| Language learning | Business English for non-native executives |
| Productivity | Quick decision-making frameworks for first-time entrepreneurs |
| Memory (looks too narrow) | Speed reading and advanced memorisation (240k buyers) (~01:32) |

## 5b. How the course's tool structures a pocket (Workbook 2, p.9 and p.28)

From the Synthesise AI screenshot and text in [Workbook 2](../workbooks/workbook-02-notes.md):

- **Data sources it claims to scan:** Google, Amazon and online communities. (WB2 p.9)
- **Output:** 20 profitable pockets, sorted by profit potential. "Don't know which to pick? Go with
  the highest." (WB2 p.9)
- **Each pocket card contains** (WB2 p.28):
  - a product promise: "I help [who] [get result] without [pain]…"
  - **Industry** and **Niche** (e.g. Parenting → Helping children build reliable morning routines)
  - **Specific knowledge** needed (e.g. age-appropriate task sequencing, visual cues)
  - **Problem solved** (a paragraph describing the buyer's situation)
  - **Four scores out of 10:** **Pain, Worsening, Purchasing power, Speed**, plus an overall
    score (e.g. 8.4, 8.0)
- Examples on screen: morning routines for parents of school children; finals revision for
  college students; aim consistency for competitive shooter players (gaming).

## 6. Niche selection rules (checklist)

- [ ] Start from a buyer's problem, not from your passion (02b-main ~01:26–01:27)
- [ ] Generate past the first, obvious ideas (02b-main ~01:29)
- [ ] Prefer proven, simple problems people already think about over new inventions (02a-vip ~00:23)
- [ ] It's a pain, not a pleasure; sits in wealth, health or relationships; hobbies only with care (02a-vip ~00:25–00:26)
- [ ] People are reminded of it often (daily beats weekly) (02a-vip ~00:28)
- [ ] It gets worse, fast, towards a breaking point (02a-vip ~00:30)
- [ ] Clear point A (feel, fear, avoid, self-image) and point B (02a-vip ~00:38)
- [ ] Specific person + specific problem, narrowed by a real distinguishing trait (02a-vip ~00:47; 02b-main ~01:31)
- [ ] Common enough that plenty of people have it (02a-vip ~00:46); a few dozen to a few hundred buyers is enough (02a-vip ~00:18; 02b-main ~01:30)
- [ ] Searched for + paid for + no good solution yet (02b-main ~01:33)
- [ ] Ignored by big companies (02b-main ~01:30)
- [ ] A short, realistic time to result is possible (02a-vip ~00:49)
- [ ] It can be written as a four-part product promise (02a-vip ~00:51)
- [ ] One format chosen for it (ebook, video program, digital asset, digital mind), plus its point A and point B (Day 3 homework, 03a-vip ~00:31)
- [ ] Among scored options, pick the highest (02b-main ~01:33; demo 8.9/10, 02b-main ~01:45)

## 7. Contradictions

- **"No products sold" vs "people already pay."** Pockets are "hidden topics with lots of demand and very low competition… most of them still untapped. **No digital products being sold on it**" (02b-main ~01:25) **vs** "topics that people are already paying for" (02b-main ~01:08), "already paying for" (02b-main ~01:33), "already paying to fix" (02b-main ~01:35), and "go for something that's proven" (02a-vip ~00:23). If nobody sells a product, "already paying" can't be observed.
- **How many buyers you need:** "a few dozen people… already makes a full-time income" / 37 people (02a-vip ~00:18; 01-opp ~01:55) **vs** "a few hundred people" (02b-main ~01:30) **vs** "common enough… probably millions and millions" of divorced dads (02a-vip ~00:46) **vs** Jonathan's 240,000 buyers (02b-main ~01:32).
- **Weight loss:** "not the most profitable… you got used to it very quickly" (02a-vip ~00:28) **vs** the 4-week weight-loss plan used as the model of transformation value (02a-vip ~00:40) and postpartum weight loss / buckwheat diet as model pockets (02b-main ~01:30–01:31).
- **Simple, obvious ideas vs non-obvious ideas:** "a very simple idea that people already think about" (02a-vip ~00:23) **vs** "your first ideas are almost always your worst… the not-so-obvious great ideas are what I call profitable pockets" (02b-main ~01:29–01:30).
- **Passion vs your own background:** "the market doesn't care about your passion" (02b-main ~01:26) **vs** the Synthesize start screen asking for "a skill, a method you know, a group you understand, maybe a problem you've solved in the past" (02b-main ~01:44), and Michal's first product: "I created a product which I knew about. I used my knowledge" (03a-vip ~00:49).
- **What matters most:** pain is "90%" (02a-vip ~00:27) **vs** "the product is the piece that everything else hangs on" (02b-main ~01:08) **vs** the pocket is "basically half the way" (02b-main ~01:33).
- **Unlock numbering slip:** the 20-vs-200-page point is said to be "because of the fourth unlock" (02a-vip ~00:20) but explained with speed, the fifth (02a-vip ~00:49).

> Our note: the main session (02b) never names the five unlocks. Pocket = unlock 4 (specificity) plus a demand test; "transformation map" = unlock 3; the dentist = unlock 5. A workable reading of the first contradiction is "proven spend on the general problem, but no good *specific* product for this segment" (see [../sessions/02b-develop.md](../sessions/02b-develop.md) §4.3).
